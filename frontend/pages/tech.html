IRIS

The technology behind IRIS

IRIS is a hybrid spatial-temporal deepfake detector. One network studies each face frame for pixel-level mistakes, and a second one watches how those frames change over time. Together they give a verdict, a confidence score and a heatmap of what looks wrong.

From upload to verdict

  1. Upload

    You send a video or image through the web interface.

  2. Frame extraction

    The video is split into frames at a fixed rate.

    OpenCV

  3. Face detection

    A face is located in every frame. Frames without a face are skipped and counted.

    MediaPipe

  4. Crop and align

    The face is cut out, the background is dropped and each crop is resized (for example 224 × 224).

    OpenCV

  5. Spatial analysis

    A CNN turns each face crop into a feature vector that captures blending, texture and lighting.

    ResNeXt

  6. Temporal analysis

    A recurrent network reads the vectors in order and looks for jitter and unnatural motion.

    LSTM

  7. Aggregation

    Per-frame scores are combined into one score using a mean, trimmed mean or top-k mean.

  8. Verdict

    You get Real or Fake, a confidence value, a per-frame score plot and heatmaps.

    Grad-CAM

The detection model

Spatial branch: ResNeXt CNN

A pretrained ResNeXt is fine-tuned on face crops. It splits each block into many parallel paths (its cardinality), which gives stronger features at a similar cost. It catches blurred jawline boundaries, checkerboard artifacts and mismatched lighting.

ResNeXtFine-tuningGrad-CAM heatmaps

Temporal branch: LSTM / GRU

A recurrent network with gated memory reads the frame features in sequence. It exposes flickering, temporal jitter and lip or eye movement that does not follow natural human mechanics, which single-frame classifiers cannot see.

LSTMGRURNN

Lightweight variant

For faster inference, the CNN can be swapped for a smaller backbone. This variant pairs a pretrained MobileNetV2 with first-order temporal differencing (Δx), which isolates frame-to-frame change, and a GRU for the sequence. EfficientNet, Xception and ViT backbones are also supported for fine-tuning.

Training and evaluation

Data

Subsets of three datasets. Splits are made per video, so frames from one video never appear in both training and test sets.

FaceForensics++DFDC sampleDeepfake-Eval-2024

Training

A pretrained backbone is fine-tuned with binary cross-entropy and the Adam optimiser. Augmentation imitates real-world quality loss.

FlipsJPEG compressionBlurNoiseColour jitter

Evaluation

ROC-AUC is the headline metric. IRIS also reports a cross-dataset test and a re-encoding test to show how well it handles unseen data.

AUCPrecisionRecallF1Confusion matrix

A decision aid, not a final judge

When too few frames contain a clear face, IRIS answers "insufficient evidence" instead of guessing. It returns a probability, not a certainty, and it works best on the kinds of manipulation it was trained on.
